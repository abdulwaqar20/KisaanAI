SOURCE: Rehman, M.A., Ali, S., Afzal, M.B.S., Khan, M.N. and Ali, M. (2020). Efficacy of Chemicals and Botanical Extracts to Control Citrus Canker on Kinnow in Sargodha Region. Volume 6, Issue 2, pp. 101-107. https://researcherslinks.com/current-issues/Efficacy-of-Chemicals-and-Botanical-Extracts/27/1/3448

Citrus canker is a serious bacterial disease of citrus, including Kinnow mandarin, caused by the bacterium Xanthomonas axonopodis pv. citri. In citrus growing areas, copper-based and streptomycin-based sprays have been used against citrus canker.

Citrus canker spray treatments and rates for citrus (Kinnow mandarin), field trial at the Citrus Research Institute, Sargodha, Punjab (2011-13), on ten-year-old bearing Kinnow trees, each amount mixed in 10 litres of water. Copper oxychloride at 3 g in 10 litres of water. Streptomycin sulphate at 1 g in 10 litres of water. Difenoconazole at 0.5 ml in 10 litres of water. Neemsol at 5 ml in 10 litres of water. Extracts of Acacia nilotica, Datura alba and Allium cepa (onion) at 10 ml each in 10 litres of water. An untreated control was kept. Foliar sprays were applied four times a year, in March-April and August-September.

Citrus canker control results on Kinnow in the Sargodha trial: copper oxychloride controlled the disease by up to 74 percent and difenoconazole by up to 56 percent. Among plant extracts, onion (Allium cepa) extract gave 74 percent control, Datura alba 64 percent, neem oil 59 percent and Acacia nilotica 52 percent. The authors recommend copper oxychloride, difenoconazole and extracts of onion (Allium cepa) and Datura alba to citrus growers against canker in Kinnow orchards during the critical period.
